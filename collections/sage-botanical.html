    The type

    Two typefaces, three sizes

    Shown here in the collection's own inks. Nothing else is used anywhere in the suite — that restraint is what makes the pieces read as a set.

    Headings — Sorrel Display

    Eleanor & Rhys

    Used for names, mastheads and welcome signs. Set at 42pt and above so the thin strokes hold.

    Names & accents — Amelia Script

    Table Four · The Orchard

    Place cards, table names and the one italic line on each piece. Never used for a full sentence.

    Body & details — Larkin Sans

    Saturday the twelfth of September, two thousand and twenty-six. Llanwern Manor, Newport. Ceremony at half past one, carriages at midnight. Black tie optional. Kindly reply by the twelfth of July.

    Everything that has to be read at arm's length: dates, addresses, menus, seating. Set at 9pt with generous leading.

    Is it right for your day?

    Where Sage Botanical works

    We would rather talk you into a different collection than sell you the wrong one.

    Barns & marquees

    Sage holds its own against exposed timber and hessian without tipping into rustic cliché.

    Gardens & orangeries

    Made for it. The botanical line work echoes real planting rather than competing with it.

    Country houses

    Works beautifully in daylight. Under heavy chandeliers the sage can read grey — we'd push the deep olive further.

    Less suited to

    Black-tie city venues and winter weddings after dark. Minimalist Monogram carries better in low light.

    Pairs naturally with eucalyptus, olive, dried grasses, ivory roses and anything in unbleached linen. Fights slightly with hot pink, primary red and gold foil — though sage with brushed brass is a combination worth seeing.
